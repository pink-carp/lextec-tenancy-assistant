Evaluation: how accurate is each version?

Back to the tool

In short. We measured whether the Swiss open model Apertus can write correct legal text for social workers. Even with confirmed facts, rule filters and two automatic checks, about one in four AI-written legal sentences was wrong. The live version therefore shows checked content for nine common situations and uses Apertus for what it does reliably: reading the case, pre-filling facts, suggesting the situation and summarising. In a blind review of 11 cases, it had no wrong legal statements and the right lane in all cases. A version with a strong commercial model (OpenAI gpt-5.6-sol) and no pre-written text also wrote the law correctly, but asked many ill-fitting questions.

5. Method and limits

  • All judges are AI reviewers (Claude agents), one reviewer per test, not lawyers. Legal points were checked against the official German text (fedlex, in corpus/law_paragraphs.jsonl).
  • All tests use known cases; the live version's situation content was written with these cases in view. A test on new, unseen cases is still to be done.
  • The checked situation content was drafted from the German text by Claude and checked in a second pass; it has not been reviewed by a lawyer.
  • Prices for the OpenAI model are third-party estimates; Apertus prices are Public AI's list prices (USD 0.82 / 2.92 per million input / output tokens).
  • Social-worker steps (confirming facts and situations, answering yes/no questions) were simulated by Claude from the client's words.
