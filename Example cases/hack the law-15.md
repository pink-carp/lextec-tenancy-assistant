We’re a family with two small children (1 and 4 years old) in **Zurich-Oerlikon**. The heating hasn’t worked for ten days and it’s only 14 degrees in the flat. We phoned the property management twice and a technician came once, but nothing has changed. Now they say there are supply problems with the spare part. We don’t know what to do and feel completely helpless. Our youngest already has a cold. Are we allowed to pay less rent?

### **Short case summary**

You live in Zurich-Oerlikon with children aged **1 and 4**. Your heating has failed for **ten days**, leaving the flat at **14°C**. Management knows about the problem, has sent a technician, and now cites spare-part supply problems. There has been no effective repair.

**On these facts, you have a strong claim for a rent reduction and urgent remedial action. But do not simply deduct an amount from your next rent payment.** The safest approach is to demand repair and a retrospective reduction in writing, while continuing to pay—or following the formal rent-deposit procedure explained below.

1. **Your legal rights**

**Article 256 OR** requires the landlord to maintain the flat in a condition suitable for its agreed use. Under **Articles 259a and 259d OR**, a defect that impairs that use entitles you to demand repair and a proportionate rent reduction.

A sustained temperature of **14°C in normally occupied rooms is a substantial impairment**, not merely a comfort issue. The Swiss Tenants’ Association’s heating guidance uses approximately **20–21°C** as the normal benchmark, with some variation for building type. That is practical guidance, not a universal statutory temperature limit.

**Spare-part shortages do not remove your rent-reduction entitlement.** Reduction compensates for the diminished use of the flat; it does not depend on proving that the landlord caused the breakdown. Supply difficulties may affect what permanent repair is feasible, but management should investigate an effective temporary heating solution rather than simply leave you waiting.

Your children’s ages reinforce the urgency. You do **not** need to prove that the heating failure caused your youngest child’s cold to claim a rent reduction.

2. **From when—and how much—can rent be reduced?**

**The reduction runs from when the landlord or its management learned of the defect until the impairment is remedied.** Your first telephone call can establish that knowledge; a written complaint is not a prerequisite for starting the reduction period. The difficulty is proving the call’s date and what you reported. The technician’s visit and management’s spare-part explanation help corroborate knowledge.

The Federal Supreme Court confirmed in **BGE 142 III 557** that a reduction can also be claimed retrospectively, even after repair. **Paying the full rent now does not, by itself, prevent you from claiming a refund later.** State expressly that you reserve that claim.

**There is no fixed statutory percentage for 14°C.** The amount depends on which rooms are affected, temperatures throughout the day, duration, and whether temporary heating restores normal use. The reduction applies to the affected period—not automatically to the entire month. A complete boiler failure does not automatically justify a 100% reduction if the flat remains partly usable.

I would pursue a meaningful reduction, but would not promise a defensible percentage without the room-by-room evidence and rent breakdown.

3. **What to do today**  
1. **Protect the children’s health first.** Contact your paediatrician about your youngest’s symptoms and the cold living conditions. If necessary, arrange somewhere warm temporarily; do not wait for the legal process. Use only suitable electric heaters safely, out of children’s reach—never an oven or combustion appliance to heat the flat.  
2. **Send an email immediately and the same notice by registered post** to management, identifying the landlord and flat. Record the failure date, both calls, the technician’s visit, the continuing 14°C temperature, and the children’s ages. Ask for acknowledgment.  
3. **Demand effective temporary heating urgently**, alongside a firm permanent-repair plan. Given ten days at 14°C, requesting an effective interim solution within **24 hours of receipt** is a reasonable practical starting point—not a statutory deadline or a guaranteed judicial finding. Set an explicit date and time, and allow access for the work.  
4. **Claim a retrospective rent reduction expressly**, from the earliest date management knew until normal use is restored. Once the evidence is assembled, specify the percentage and period claimed.  
5. **Document temperatures several times daily** in the living room and both children’s sleeping rooms. Record date, time, room and temperature; photograph the thermometer away from windows, heaters and direct sunlight. Keep correspondence, call logs, technician details and receipts. Ask management for the technician’s report.

Evidence of the actual cold matters. In **PD140009**, the Zurich Higher Court explained that proving a heating system was not running did not itself establish inadequate room temperatures. A temperature log is therefore much stronger than simply reporting a broken boiler.

4. **If management still does not act**

**Do not use an informal rent deduction as pressure.** If the landlord disputes the amount, you risk arrears and ultimately termination under **Article 257d OR**. Continue paying in full with an express reservation unless a reduction is agreed or you properly use the statutory deposit route.

Under **Articles 259g–259h OR**, you can deposit rent officially (*Mietzinshinterlegung*):

* First set a **reasonable written deadline** for remedying the defect and threaten official deposit if it expires without remedy.  
* Give the landlord written notice of the deposit.  
* Obtain the official instructions and deposit **rent that becomes due in the future**, rather than withholding it or moving it to your own account.  
* Bring your claims before the conciliation authority **within 30 days of the due date of the first deposited rent**. Missing that deadline causes the deposited funds to fall to the landlord.

Properly deposited rent counts as paid. Zurich Courts explain that the conciliation authority issues a deposit instruction for payment to the district court’s cashier.

For Oerlikon, contact the **Zurich District Court’s tenancy conciliation authority** (*Schlichtungsbehörde in Miet- und Pachtsachen*): **058 111 74 01**. Its published tenancy legal-advice number is **058 111 74 74**, available Monday 09:00–11:30 and 13:30–16:00, and Wednesday 13:30–16:00. Ask specifically about urgent heating defects and the deposit procedure. You can pursue repair and reduction through conciliation without first depositing rent.

5. **Extra costs and the facts still missing**

Under **Article 259e OR**, necessary losses caused by the defect may be recoverable unless the landlord proves absence of fault. Keep evidence of reasonable heater hire, additional electricity and any necessary temporary accommodation. **Reimbursement is not automatic**: necessity, causation, amount and fault matter. Spare-part shortages may be relevant here, unlike for the rent reduction. Ask management to approve costs in writing where feasible, without delaying essential health protection.

Do not commission major heating repairs yourself or terminate the tenancy without specific advice. **Article 259b OR** has different conditions for substitute repair and immediate termination.

The following facts are needed to quantify and pursue the claim safely:

| Missing fact | Why it matters |
| ----- | ----- |
| Exact breakdown date and first notification date | Establishes the reduction period. |
| What you told management, and any written response | Proves knowledge of the continuing defect. |
| Temperatures by room and time; any working temporary heaters | Establishes severity and any improvement. |
| Monthly net rent, separate charges and next rent due date | Enables calculation and safe deposit planning. |
| Names of the contractual landlord and all tenants | Ensures the notice and claim identify the correct parties. |
| Additional expenses and any medical assessment | Supports a separate damages claim, where justified. |

**Bottom line:** you do not have to accept indefinite living conditions at 14°C. Demand urgent temporary heating and claim a retrospective reduction now; preserve payment discipline while you enforce those rights.
