CASE 8 \- lane 3 

My name is Fatima and I’ve lived in a flat in **Zurich-Wiedikon** for six years. Three weeks ago I emailed the property management that there is mould in the bathroom and water standing in the cellar. Last week a termination for the end of March arrived by registered mail, without any reason. I’ve never caused problems and always paid on time. I think this is because of my report. Can I fight it? I didn’t fully understand the deadline. 

Follow up questions:

- Has the property management replied to your email or taken any action (inspection, repair, contractor visit)? What exactly did they say or do, and when?  
- Is the lease for an unlimited or fixed period, what notice period and termination dates does it state, and is the end of March the first possible date under it?  
- Do other tenants in the building also have a termination?

**Yes—Fatima has a potentially credible basis to challenge the termination, but retaliation is not yet established. So the case needs to be handed over to human legal assistance.**

Under **Art. 271a(1)(a) OR**, a landlord’s termination is challengeable if it was issued because the tenant asserted tenancy rights in good faith. Reporting mould and standing water may support that ground; the exact email and the landlord’s response matter.

**The urgent issue is the 30-day deadline:** under **Art. 273(1) OR**, the challenge must be submitted to the tenancy conciliation authority within 30 days after receipt—not within 30 days before the March termination date. Do not wait for all the evidence before protecting that deadline.

### **Case summary**

Based solely on Fatima’s account:

* **Tenancy:** residential flat in Zurich-Wiedikon, occupied for six years.  
* **Defect report:** three weeks ago, she emailed management about bathroom mould and standing water in the cellar.  
* **Termination:** last week, a registered termination notice arrived, ending the tenancy at the end of March; the year and exact dates are unspecified.  
* **Reason:** none was given.  
* **Tenant’s position:** rent has always been paid on time, with no known problems or warnings.  
* **Suspected motive:** retaliation for reporting the defects.  
* **Objective:** retain the flat and understand the challenge deadline.

**Unresolved:** the legally relevant receipt date, formal validity of the notice, contractual notice requirements, actual termination motive and supporting evidence.

### **Two important clarifications**

**No reason given does not, by itself, invalidate the notice.** Under **Art. 271(2) OR**, Fatima can demand the reason. That request does not suspend the challenge deadline.

**Close timing is evidence, not automatic proof.** In **4A\_345/2007**, the Federal Supreme Court explained that, outside statutory protected periods, the tenant generally must establish the facts showing an abusive termination, while the terminating party must contribute to clarifying its reasons. Fatima’s email, the short interval and any inconsistent explanation therefore matter; six years of timely payment alone does not prevent an ordinary termination.

### **Deadline and immediate next step**

The 30-day period starts **the day after legally effective receipt**. With registered mail, receipt may occur when collection first becomes possible—generally the day after the collection slip is delivered—not when the letter is actually collected. The Zurich Higher Court confirmed this distinction in **LF190080**. **Do not assume that the seven-day postal collection period extends the challenge deadline.**

For a flat in Zurich-Wiedikon, the challenge belongs before the **tenancy conciliation authority at the Zurich District Court**. Fatima should promptly file a signed application challenging the termination as retaliatory, with an alternative request for an extension if appropriate, and separately request management’s written reasons.

**The exact last filing date cannot be calculated from “last week.”** Obtain the delivery details first and act on the earliest plausible receipt date. If the deadline may already have passed, urgently assess formal invalidity rather than assuming every route is closed.
