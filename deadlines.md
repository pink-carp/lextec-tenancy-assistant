Calculate deadlines by identifying the applicable legal rule first. **Never apply one universal calculation to all tenancy cases.**

**1\. Classify the deadline**

Distinguish:

* **Notice periods:** calculate backwards from a permissible termination date.  
* **Substantive deadlines:** periods for asserting rights under the OR, such as challenging termination.  
* **Procedural deadlines:** periods for taking action before an authority or court under the ZPO.  
* **Limitation periods:** assess separately under the OR, including possible interruption.

**2\. Establish the triggering event**

Identify the document, delivery method, relevant recipient and legally effective receipt date. Distinguish the document’s date, posting date, delivery date and reading date.

* **Private termination notices:** generally apply the absolute receipt principle. For uncollected registered notices, receipt generally occurs when collection first becomes possible—not automatically after seven days.  
* **Payment demands under Art. 257d OR and rent-increase notices:** apply the relative receipt principle, generally actual collection or expiry of the seven-day collection period.  
* **Court documents:** apply Arts. 137–138 ZPO. The seven-day deemed-service rule requires that the recipient had to expect service.  
* **Ordinary court mail delivered on weekends or recognised holidays:** apply Art. 142(1bis) ZPO; deemed communication occurs on the next working day.

Verify the legislation applicable on the event date, particularly any amendments affecting weekend delivery of private notices.

**3\. Apply the correct calculation**

* **Day-based periods:** exclude the triggering day; count calendar days from the following day.  
* **Month-based periods:** use the corresponding calendar date, not multiples of 30 days. Do not add an extra day. If the corresponding date does not exist, use the month’s last day.  
* **Final-day adjustments:** apply Art. 78 OR and the relevant Saturday legislation, or Art. 142(3) ZPO, as appropriate.  
* **Public holidays:** use the legally relevant location; under Art. 142(3) ZPO, this is the court’s location.  
* **Backwards-calculated notice requirements:** do not automatically move the cut-off to the next working day if that would shorten the required notice.

Main calculation provisions: **Arts. 76–78 OR; Arts. 142–143 ZPO**.

**4\. Recognise the principal deadlines**

| Action | Deadline | Provision |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Challenge initial rent | 30 days after taking possession | Art. 270 OR |
| Challenge rent increase | 30 days after notification | Art. 270b OR |
| Challenge termination | 30 days after receipt | Art. 273(1) OR |
| Request extension of an open-ended tenancy | 30 days after receipt of termination | Art. 273(2)(a) OR |
| Request extension of a fixed-term tenancy | At least 60 days before expiry | Art. 273(2)(b) OR |
| Request a second extension | At least 60 days before the first expires | Art. 273(3) OR |
| Assert claims following official rent deposit | 30 days from the due date of the first deposited rent instalment | Art. 259h OR |
| Payment grace period for residential or commercial rent arrears | At least 30 days after receipt of the qualifying demand | Art. 257d(1) OR |
| Termination following unpaid arrears | Separate notice of at least 30 days to month-end | Art. 257d(2) OR |
| Reject a conciliation authority’s proposed decision | 20 days after written notification | Art. 211 ZPO |
| File a tenancy claim after authorisation to proceed | 30 days after notification | Art. 209(4) ZPO |
| Request written reasons for an unreasoned decision | 10 days after notification | Art. 239(2) ZPO |
| Appeal or complaint | Generally 30 days; often 10 days in summary proceedings or other specified cases | Arts. 311, 314 and 321 ZPO |

For rent-reduction requests, separately apply the landlord’s **30-day response period** and the tenant’s subsequent **30-day conciliation period** under **Art. 270a(2) OR**.

For ordinary termination and rent-increase notification, check the lease, permissible termination dates and **Arts. 266a–266e and 269d OR**.

**5\. Check court-holiday suspension**

Under Arts. 145–146 ZPO, applicable procedural periods pause:

* Seven days before through seven days after Easter.  
* 15 July–15 August inclusive.  
* 18 December–2 January inclusive.

Do **not** apply this suspension to:

* Substantive OR challenge periods.  
* Conciliation proceedings.  
* Summary proceedings.

**Important:** the 30-day court-filing period after authorisation to proceed under Art. 209(4) ZPO generally **is suspended**, although the authorisation comes from the conciliation authority.

**6\. Determine what counts as timely action**

* **Court submissions:** timely handover to Swiss Post may preserve the deadline under Art. 143 ZPO; electronic filing must meet the applicable requirements.  
* **Private notices:** timely posting alone generally does not suffice; receipt matters.  
* **Payments:** apply the relevant payment rule. Do not transfer the special rule for payments to a court to ordinary rent payments.  
* Statutory procedural deadlines cannot be extended; court-set deadlines may be extendable under Art. 144 ZPO.  
* Restoration under Art. 148 ZPO requires a separate assessment. Do not assume it rescues a missed substantive OR deadline.  
* Treat limitation separately: Arts. 128, 132 and 135 OR address rent claims, calculation and interruption. An ordinary creditor reminder alone does not interrupt limitation.

**7\. Handle uncertainty and display the result**

For every deadline, show the social worker:

* Required action and legal basis.  
* Triggering event and evidence.  
* Legally effective receipt date.  
* Calculation, including any suspension or final-day adjustment.  
* Final deadline and required submission method.  
* Missing facts, uncertainties and the law version used.

If information is missing, continue the supported parts of the assessment. Ask targeted questions and provide conditional calculations where useful. Label the earliest plausible deadline as a precautionary planning date, **not a verified legal deadline**.

Escalate imminent or potentially missed deadlines without waiting for all documents. Do not conclude that every right is lost merely because one deadline may have expired.

Supporting authorities: **BGE 140 III 244, E. 5** on receipt and the substantive nature of the termination-challenge period; **BGE 138 III 615, E. 2** on suspension after authorisation to proceed; **5A\_691/2023, E. 5.5–5.6** on month-based calculation.
