## **User-Facing Disclosure**

## **What does the AI do?**

We have created an AI-supported tenancy information tool designed to help social workers assist people experiencing residential housing problems in Switzerland.

It can help to:

* Understand and organise the information you provide about your housing situation.  
* Identify possible tenancy-law issues and relevant Swiss legal rules.  
* Ask questions to clarify important facts.  
* Identify possible deadlines and explain why they may matter.  
* Prepare a summary of your situation and suggest possible next steps.  
* Identify when legal advice or other specialist support may be needed.  
* Find relevant legal, social and support services.

The tool is designed primarily for private residential tenancy matters in the Canton of Zurich.

The tool assesses the information you share with the social worker about your housing situation. It helps determine what kind of support is appropriate by placing your case into one of three lanes:

* **Lane 1 — AI assistance:** If your situation is relatively straightforward and there is enough information to assess it, the tool provides relevant tenancy information and potential next steps to help you understand your situation.  
* **Lane 2 — More information needed:** If important details are missing or unclear, the tool continues assessing what it can and identifies what further information is needed before clearer guidance can be given. The social worker relays what additional information is necessary.  
* **Lane 3 — Human legal support:** If your situation is urgent, particularly serious, or involves circumstances that require specialist legal judgement, the tool highlights that referral to a qualified human lawyer or appropriate specialist service may be appropriate.

The social worker oversees the process, reviews the tool's assessment and helps determine the appropriate next steps. If your situation requires urgent human support, this referral will not be delayed while the tool gathers more information..

**Liability Waiver:**

DISCLAIMER: AI-generated information can contain inaccurate or outdated content, omit relevant legal requirements, or fail to identify important deadlines. This tool aims to provide general information about, and connect you with residential tenancy rights and procedures in Switzerland. It can assist you in:

- Understanding your housing and tenancy situation;  
- Identifying potentially relevant legal issues to your circumstance;  
- Considering the general rights, obligations, and possible remedies you are entitled to as a matter of law;  
- Noting important dates, deadlines, or procedural steps that may require action on your end or behalf;  
- Recognizing when your situation necessitates professional advice, urgent legal assistance, or referral to social support.  
- Finding helpful legal, social, and support services for your situation.

**When a Human Becomes Involved?**

A social worker is involved in using the tool and reviews the information it produces. Furthermore the social worker is asked to give a vulnerability assessment (score from 1-10) based on his own assessment

If your situation is unclear, the tool may ask further questions before suggesting next steps.

If your case involves a serious legal risk, an important deadline, a possible loss of your home or a complicated legal issue, the tool may recommend that you speak with a qualified legal professional or specialist service.

If there is an immediate risk to your safety or housing, the social worker should prioritise appropriate urgent human assistance. You should not wait for an AI-generated response in an emergency.

You can also ask for human assistance at any time, even if the tool has not recommended it.

**Data Protection Law and AI Act related questions**

## Below is an operational guideline adapted to a Zurich state social service using an AI tool supplied by Lextec, a private startup. It distinguishes the authority’s responsibilities from Lextec’s obligations and treats the proposed safeguards as internal operating rules, rather than implying that every safeguard is expressly required by statute.

## The principal legal basis is Zurich’s Information and Data Protection Act (IDG): § 8 governs lawful processing, § 6 governs outsourcing, § 7 governs information security, and § 10 governs impact assessment and prior review. The authority remains responsible even where Lextec processes data on its behalf.

## **1\. Purpose and permitted use**

The Lextec AI tool may support social workers in collecting relevant facts, identifying questions and recommending a referral pathway or “lane” for counselling and support services.

The tool is a **decision-support system, not a decision-maker**. It must not independently determine entitlement to social assistance, deny access to services, close a case or issue a legally binding decision.

The approved use is limited to structured questioning and triage. The tool must not provide case-specific legal advice to service users. Cases requiring legal interpretation must be referred to an appropriately qualified person.

A disclaimer does not replace lawful data processing, effective human oversight or quality assurance.

## **2\. Responsibilities**

| Role | Responsibility |
| ----- | ----- |
| Social service management | Approve the intended use, establish the legal basis, allocate resources and authorise deployment. The authority retains responsibility for its information processing and public-service decisions. |
| Internal system owner | Maintain the approved configuration, user permissions, operating procedures, supplier documentation and incident process. |
| Data protection and information security leads | Review data flows, outsourcing arrangements, security controls, retention rules and the data protection impact assessment; coordinate any required prior review. |
| Social worker | Enter only necessary information, critically review recommendations, identify urgent circumstances and document the final referral decision. |
| Qualified legal reviewer | Validate legally relevant triage criteria, review escalated cases and assess recurring legal errors. |
| Lextec | Supply and maintain the tool within the agreed scope, disclose relevant technical dependencies, implement contracted safeguards, support testing and incident investigation, and refrain from unauthorised data use. |

Lextec’s role must be established from the actual deployment:

* If Lextec only supplies software and cannot access case data, it is not automatically a processor of those data.  
* If Lextec hosts the tool or accesses case data through support, maintenance, logs or diagnostics, the authority must assess and regulate that processing as outsourcing.  
* Lextec must not use case data for its own product development, analytics or model training under the existing triage authorisation. Any proposed independent use requires a separate legal assessment and approval.

## **3\. Conditions before deployment**

The tool must not be used with identifiable real-case data until the following conditions have been met:

1. The intended purpose, permitted functions and prohibited uses are documented.  
2. The competent legal team has identified the statutory basis for processing, including any special personal data.  
3. A data protection impact assessment has been completed, and any required prior review by the competent data protection supervisory authority has taken place.  
4. Hosting, data flows, external interfaces, remote access, telemetry, logs and subprocessors have been documented and approved.  
5. The authority and Lextec have agreed the necessary contractual safeguards.  
6. Triage rules and legally relevant outputs have been tested and approved by qualified reviewers.  
7. Users have completed training and have access to an escalation route.  
8. Information notices, record-keeping rules and incident procedures are ready.

Until these conditions are satisfied, testing should use synthetic or genuinely anonymised cases only.

## **4\. Lawful processing and confidentiality**

### **4.1 Applicable framework**

The social service’s processing is governed principally by Zurich cantonal information and data protection law, together with the legislation governing its public task.

Lextec’s private-sector status does not remove the authority’s cantonal obligations. Federal data protection requirements must also be assessed for Lextec’s processing where applicable, particularly any processing for its own purposes.

### **4.2 Special personal data**

Information about health, mental health and measures of social assistance must be treated as special personal data. Other information or combinations of information may also qualify because of their sensitivity or the way they are analysed.

Before deployment, the authority must identify a sufficiently specific basis in a formal statute for the intended processing of special personal data. A signed consent form is not a substitute for that assessment.

Where consent is relied on for a separately assessed disclosure or optional activity, it must be informed, specific and genuinely voluntary. Dependence on the social service must be taken into account. Refusal of an optional AI-supported workflow must not itself disadvantage the person.

### **4.3 Official secrecy**

Staff must preserve official secrecy. Disclosure to Lextec or its personnel must be legally permissible and restricted to the approved purpose and necessary recipients.

Confidentiality clauses alone do not make an otherwise prohibited disclosure lawful. Lextec personnel and subprocessors must be subject to appropriate confidentiality commitments, access restrictions and training.

## **5\. Information for service users**

Before personal information is entered into the tool, the social worker must provide an accessible explanation covering:

* the use and purpose of AI-assisted triage;  
* the categories of information processed;  
* the responsible authority and contact point;  
* whether Lextec or other providers can access the information;  
* relevant hosting locations and any approved foreign access;  
* the limits of the tool and the human review process;  
* retention arrangements; and  
* how to request access, correction or review through the applicable procedure.

Information must be adapted to the person’s language and comprehension needs. A general legal disclaimer is not an adequate substitute for a privacy notice.

The tool’s interface must also clearly identify AI-generated recommendations to staff.

## **6\. Data entry and technical safeguards**

### **6.1 Minimum necessary information**

Social workers must:

* use a case reference rather than a name wherever possible;  
* omit addresses, identification numbers, employer names and other identifiers unless demonstrably necessary;  
* describe relevant facts without unnecessary intimate or medical detail;  
* avoid uploading entire case files when a limited factual summary is sufficient; and  
* check documents for information about unrelated third parties before submission.

Pseudonymised information remains personal data where the person can still be identified. Removing a name alone does not make a case anonymous.

### **6.2 Approved environment only**

Case information must not be copied into unapproved public AI services or personal accounts.

The approved environment must include appropriate access controls, secure authentication, protection of data in storage and transmission, traceable access and a controlled maintenance process.

Local hosting does not, by itself, establish that no foreign disclosure occurs. Remote support, external model calls, telemetry, diagnostic services and backup arrangements must be checked separately.

## **7\. Human review and escalation**

Every referral recommendation must receive **substantive human review before it affects the person’s access to counselling or support**.

The social worker must:

1. Check that the factual summary is accurate and complete.  
2. Assess whether the suggested pathway matches the person’s circumstances and expressed needs.  
3. Check for urgency, vulnerability, missing facts and inconsistent reasoning.  
4. Override or disregard an unsuitable recommendation.  
5. Record the final human decision and its reasons.

A final confirmation click does not constitute meaningful review.

Because social workers may lack legal training, legally significant issues must be escalated to a qualified reviewer. Escalation is mandatory where:

* the recommendation depends on a disputed legal interpretation;  
* a deadline, eviction risk or potential loss of rights is involved;  
* the recommendation would restrict or delay access to support;  
* the facts are incomplete, contradictory or outside the validated scope; or  
* the social worker cannot independently assess the recommendation.

Urgent protective action and referrals must not be delayed while waiting for the AI tool or a routine review.

The tool must not autonomously infer emotions from voice, facial expressions or similar biometric signals. Social workers may record their own relevant observations, but must distinguish observations from unsupported assumptions.

## **8\. Quality assurance and staff training**

Lextec and the social service must test the tool before launch and after material changes to the model, prompts, legal sources or triage rules.

Testing must cover:

* typical and borderline tenancy and social-support cases;  
* urgent cases and cases with incomplete information;  
* incorrect or fabricated legal references;  
* inappropriate reassurance and missed deadlines;  
* discriminatory or inconsistent referral outcomes; and  
* the risk of a person being placed in a lower-support pathway incorrectly.

Qualified legal reviewers must validate legally relevant criteria and outputs. Acceptance criteria and test results must be documented before approval.

Staff training must cover the tool’s scope, hallucinations, automation bias, confidentiality, data minimisation, verification and escalation. Staff must know how to continue the service without the tool.

## **9\. Records, retention and deletion**

The authority must distinguish between:

* the official case record;  
* temporary AI inputs and outputs;  
* access and security logs;  
* diagnostic records;  
* caches and retrieval databases; and  
* backups.

The final referral decision and sufficient information to explain it must be retained in the official case record under the applicable record-keeping and archival rules. AI output must be labelled as such and must not be stored as an established fact without verification.

Temporary AI data must be deleted according to an approved retention schedule. Lextec must explain how deletion applies to logs, caches, retrieval databases and backups, including any delayed deletion cycles.

Do not delete information indiscriminately where retention, an access request, an incident investigation or another preservation duty requires it. Conversely, auditability does not justify retaining all sensitive inputs indefinitely.

## **10\. Contractual requirements for Lextec**

The supplier agreement must address, as applicable:

* documented processing instructions and permitted purposes;  
* a prohibition on unauthorised training, reuse, sale or disclosure of case data;  
* hosting locations, remote access and external services;  
* approval and oversight of subprocessors;  
* confidentiality and personnel access restrictions;  
* security controls and evidence of compliance;  
* incident notification without undue delay;  
* assistance with access, correction, deletion and supervisory inquiries;  
* audit and inspection rights;  
* quality assurance, updates and change control;  
* retention, return, deletion and exit arrangements; and  
* service continuity, liability and responsibility for defects.

The agreement must not suggest that outsourcing transfers the authority’s statutory responsibility to Lextec.

## **11\. Incidents and suspension**

Staff must promptly report suspected data disclosures, inaccurate recommendations, discriminatory outcomes and failures of human oversight through the internal incident process.

The authority must assess corrective action, preservation of necessary evidence and any applicable notification duties. Lextec must assist under the supplier agreement.

The system owner must suspend the affected function where a serious confidentiality risk, repeated harmful triage error or inability to exercise meaningful human oversight is identified. A manual service pathway must remain available.

## **12\. EU AI Act assessment**

For a tool used solely by a Zurich social service, with no relevant EU market or output-use connection, the EU AI Act will generally not be the directly applicable framework. Lextec and the authority must nevertheless document the territorial assessment and reassess it if deployment changes.

For any EU deployment or other relevant EU connection:

* assess the intended purpose and the actual effect on access to services;  
* examine whether the tool evaluates eligibility for, or materially affects, access to essential public assistance benefits or services;  
* do not assume that describing the tool as “triage” or adding human approval excludes high-risk classification;  
* assess prohibited practices separately, including exploitation of vulnerability; and  
* establish the respective provider and deployer obligations and applicable implementation dates before deployment.

Regardless of direct EU applicability, transparency, staff competence, effective human oversight and documented risk management are mandatory internal safeguards under this guideline.

## **13\. Review and approval**

The guideline must be reviewed at least annually and whenever the intended use, applicable law, supplier arrangements, model, data flows or triage criteria materially change.

Before final approval, complete the authority-specific statutory basis, approved referral pathways, escalation contacts, retention schedule and Lextec deployment details.
