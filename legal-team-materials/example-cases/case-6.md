CASE 6 \- lane 3

Hello, I am Maria and I don’t know what to do. Together with my husband and four kids we live in our apartment in **Zurich-Altstetten**. My husband works in a warehouse, I clean part-time. We already receive premium subsidies. Last week a letter came from the property management saying our rent will go up by 280 francs from January. We currently pay 1,850. I didn’t read the letter closely because we were so shocked. We can’t afford this and we’re afraid of losing the flat. Do we just have to accept it? 

Follow up questions: 

- What exactly does the letter say as the reason for the increase (for example reference interest rate, inflation, higher costs, renovation or yield)? Paste the wording, including any percentages, figures or dates it mentions.

**Maria does not simply have to accept the increase. This is an urgent tenancy-law referral, not an established eviction case.**

Under **Art. 269d OR**, an ordinary rent increase must be notified on the canton-approved form, give reasons, and meet the statutory notice requirements. Under **Art. 270b OR**, a tenant can challenge an abusive increase before the tenancy conciliation authority **within 30 days of notification—not 30 days before it takes effect in January**.

Because the notice reportedly arrived last week, the deadline may still be open. Obtain the notice and establish the exact receipt date immediately. Neither the increase’s size nor the family’s inability to afford it establishes, by itself, that it is unlawful; its justification must be assessed under **Arts. 269–269a OR**.

### **Missing information**

### **Obtain first: deadline and notice validity**

1. **Exact date and circumstances of receipt:** when was it delivered or collected, and by whom? Keep the envelope and any postal collection slip.  
2. **Complete notice:** all pages, attachments and any canton-approved form—not just the covering letter.  
3. **Stated reasons and calculation:** reference interest rate, inflation, increased operating costs, renovations, or another basis?  
4. **Exact effective date:** which January and which day?  
5. **Any termination or payment warning:** is one included, or has a separate notice arrived?

### **Obtain for the merits review**

6. **Lease and amendments:** ordinary, indexed or stepped rent; contractual notice period and termination dates.  
7. **Rent breakdown:** current and proposed net rent, ancillary charges and any parking or other separate charges.  
8. **Previous adjustments:** last increase or reduction, relevant notices, and the calculation bases underlying the current rent.  
9. **Tenant identities:** who signed the lease, and to whom was the increase addressed?  
10. **Any claimed improvements:** what work occurred, when, and how does management explain the allocation?

### **Obtain for financial and practical support**

11. **Payment position:** any arrears, current payment difficulties or existing tenancy dispute?  
12. **Support needs:** household income and essential expenses, existing benefits, tenancy-association membership or legal-expenses insurance, and whether German-language assistance is needed.

**Do not delay deadline protection while collecting the full financial information.**

### **Triage and immediate action**

**Priority: urgent—contact a tenancy-law specialist today or the next working day.** Ask them to:

* establish and protect the **30-day challenge deadline**;  
* check the official form, reasons and effective-date requirements;  
* assess the increase’s calculation and legal basis;  
* prepare a timely application to the **Schlichtungsbehörde in Miet- und Pachtsachen for the district of Zurich**, if appropriate.

A request to management for an explanation is **not a substitute for filing with the conciliation authority**. Do not wait for management’s reply if the deadline is approaching. Continue paying the existing rent on time; obtain specific advice about payments from the proposed effective date rather than stopping or reducing payments unilaterally.
