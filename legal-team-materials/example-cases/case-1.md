CASE 1 \- lane 1 or 2

Hi, I am Cristina and I found mold in my apartment. I live in a 3-room flat in Zurich and moved in about two years ago. Last month I noticed black spots on the wall behind the bed in the bedroom, and now it is also starting in the corner near the window. It smells bad.

I told my landlord’s property manager about it by phone, and he said it’s because I don’t ventilate enough and that I should just wipe it with vinegar. But I do open the windows every morning\!

I took a few photos on my phone. I’m so angry that I’m thinking of just paying less rent this month, maybe 30%, until they fix it. Or should I move out? I can’t afford a lawyer. What are my rights and what should I do now?

Legal reasoning: 

Cristina should **report the mould in writing now, but do not simply deduct 30% from this month’s rent.** Spreading mould and a bad smell warrant investigation—not just an assumption that you caused it. 

Under **Art. 256 of the Swiss Code of Obligations (OR)**, your landlord must keep the apartment fit for its agreed use. **Arts. 259a and 259d OR** give you rights to repairs and a proportionate rent reduction for a defect that affects use, provided you are not responsible for it and it is not something you must remedy yourself as minor maintenance.

**The manager’s accusation does not establish responsibility.** In Zurich judgment **PD190002**, the court held that the landlord had to prove its claim that the tenants caused the mould through inadequate heating or ventilation. When that proof failed, the tenants received a rent reduction.

Opening your windows every morning is relevant, but it does not settle the cause either. An inspection should assess moisture, the walls and windows, heating, ventilation and furniture placement. Do not accept blame before that assessment.

Steps to take: 

1. **Preserve evidence.** Keep the original photos, take dated close-ups and wider shots, and record the smell, spread and any effect on using the bedroom. Write down when you called, whom you spoke to and their response. Keep a brief ventilation and heating log.  
2. **Send a signed letter by registered post** to the landlord named in your lease, copied to the property manager. Attach photos and confirm the earlier phone report. Request an inspection of the cause and proper removal—not merely wiping or painting over the spots.  
3. **Set concrete deadlines.** Given the spread and smell, request an inspection within a few days and set a reasonable deadline for remedying the problem. The appropriate repair deadline depends on the extent and necessary work; ask for immediate protective measures if a full repair takes longer. Offer access for inspection.  
4. **Claim a rent reduction in writing**, starting from the date the landlord or authorised manager learned of the problem, and reserve your right to quantify it once the impact is clearer. A phone report can be relevant evidence of knowledge, but written confirmation now makes your position much easier to prove.

If you want to use the official rent-deposit procedure below, your letter must also expressly warn that you will deposit future rent with the designated authority if the repair deadline expires without remedy.

Why no reduction of 30% by yourself? 

**There is no automatic 30% reduction for mould.** The amount depends on its severity, duration and how much it limits use of the apartment. In **PD190002**, the Zurich court awarded **10%** for the mould proved in that particular case—not a standard rate for all cases.

A reduction can run from when the landlord learned of the defect until it is remedied. **Continuing to pay the full rent does not prevent you from claiming that reduction later.**

An unsupported deduction risks being treated as rent arrears. Under **Art. 257d OR**, a residential landlord can send a written payment demand giving at least **30 days**, with a termination warning, and then terminate if the arrears remain unpaid. Do not ignore such a notice if one arrives.

If the landlord still does not act: deposit rent officially

Swiss law provides a safer pressure mechanism than withholding rent yourself: **Mietzinshinterlegung**, meaning payment into the officially designated deposit account.

Under **Arts. 259g–259h OR**, you must:

* Give a **reasonable written repair deadline** and warn of depositing future rent if it expires without remedy.  
* Announce the deposit to the landlord **in writing**.  
* Obtain the correct instructions from the local tenancy conciliation authority and deposit rent that is **not yet due**, in accordance with those instructions. Keeping money in your own bank account does not count.  
* Submit your repair and rent-reduction claims to the conciliation authority **within 30 days of the due date of the first deposited rent**. Otherwise, the deposited money falls to the landlord.

**Properly deposited rent counts as paid.** Have the authority check the steps and timing before changing where you pay.

Health and Moving out

The Federal Office of Public Health warns that mould can irritate the eyes, skin and airways and worsen respiratory problems. **If you have symptoms, contact your doctor** and explain the mould exposure. If possible, sleep away from the affected area while it is assessed, particularly if you have asthma or a weakened immune system.

Do not treat the manager’s vinegar suggestion as a substitute for finding and fixing the moisture source. Avoid disturbing a spreading mould patch before getting advice; document it first.

**You do not need to decide to move out today.** Under **Art. 259b OR**, immediate termination can be available where a defect excludes or seriously impairs residential use and the landlord, knowing of it, fails to remedy it within a reasonable time. Your description alone is not enough to establish that threshold. Get advice before terminating without notice: leaving physically does not, by itself, end the lease or your rent obligations.
