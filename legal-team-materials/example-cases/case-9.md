CASE 9 \- lane 2

Hello, my boyfriend and I have lived together for three years in a 3-room flat in **Schlieren**, and we both signed the lease. We’ve now split up. He moved in with his parents last week and no longer wants to pay anything. I have our 2-year-old daughter with me. The property management has already asked why only part of the rent is arriving. I don’t know whether I can stay in the flat alone and who is liable for what. He says he has given notice. 

Follow up questions: 

- Did you sign his notice or authorise him to terminate the lease for both of you?   
- What is the most formal communication you have received from property management about the unpaid rent?   
- Can you share the lease and any notice or rent-arrears letter, including the dates you received them?   
- What does the lease say about the tenants, the notice period, the termination dates and the rent due date? Are you both named as tenants on the same lease, and is there a fixed term?  
- What is your own income, and does it cover the full rent alone? Do you receive child support, family allowances, or any other support, and does your ex-partner have any obligation to your daughter that has already been discussed or agreed?

**Moving out does not release your boyfriend from the lease, and he normally cannot terminate a jointly signed lease on his own. You can remain in the flat while the lease continues—but the full rent must still be paid.**

I am treating you as unmarried, based on your description. I have not seen the lease, his notice or the management’s correspondence, so the conclusions below are provisional.

1. **Who owes the rent to the landlord?**

Under **Art. 544(3) and Art. 144 OR**, partners renting a home jointly will generally be **jointly and severally liable**, unless a different arrangement was agreed with the landlord. This means management can demand **the entire outstanding rent from either of you**, not just half each.

His move to his parents does not end his contractual liability. Equally, paying your usual share does not protect you against a claim for the unpaid balance. The lease’s wording should be checked, particularly any clause headed *Solidarhaftung*.

2. **Does his notice end the lease?**

**Not ordinarily, if he acted only for himself and you neither signed nor authorised it.** A joint lease is one legal relationship; there is normally no separate “half” that one tenant can terminate.

Residential termination must be in writing under **Art. 266l OR**. In **BGE 140 III 491**, the Federal Supreme Court confirmed that the termination right belongs to the joint tenants collectively. A notice from him alone, without authority to act for you, would generally be ineffective.

Ask management for a copy of what he sent and its response. **Do not sign a joint termination merely to remove his name** if you want to stay: that could end the whole lease.

3. **Can you stay as the sole tenant?**

**You can continue living there under the existing joint lease while it remains in force.** His departure does not automatically require you and your daughter to leave. However, he remains a co-tenant; moving out does not itself remove his contractual rights either.

To make you the **sole contractual tenant**, the safest route is a written agreement between you, him and the landlord. It should specify:

* when you take over alone;  
* when his future liability ends;  
* who remains liable for existing arrears; and  
* how the deposit is dealt with.

Management is not generally obliged to accept this change. Because you are unmarried, the court’s power under **Art. 121 ZGB** to transfer a family-home lease in divorce does not apply merely because your daughter lives with you.

4. **What should he contribute between the two of you?**

This is **separate from the landlord’s right to collect the full rent**.

Under **Art. 148 OR**, equal shares are the starting point unless your agreement or underlying relationship provides otherwise. If you pay more than your proper share, you may have a reimbursement claim against him.

But it would be unsafe to promise that he must pay half indefinitely after moving out. Your previous cost-sharing arrangement, any separation agreement and your sole use of the flat can affect the allocation. Keep the lease, payment records and messages about rent and moving out.

**Child maintenance is a separate issue.** Under **Art. 285 ZGB**, it depends on your daughter’s needs and both parents’ financial circumstances, and also covers the care component. Her housing costs should be addressed in that assessment; maintenance is not automatically the same as his former half of the rent.

5. **Protect the tenancy now**

**The rent shortfall is the immediate risk—even if his own notice is invalid.**

1. **Contact management in writing today.** Ask for the exact arrears, a copy of his notice and confirmation of how it is treating the joint lease. If true, state clearly that you have not agreed to terminate it and want to remain.  
2. **Arrange payment of the full rent and outstanding balance if you can**, keeping proof and reserving your reimbursement claim against him. If you cannot, seek a written payment arrangement immediately and contact local social services for urgent assistance. Do not assume negotiations suspend a deadline.  
3. **Check every letter carefully.** Under **Art. 257d OR**, the landlord can give a written payment deadline of at least **30 days**, with a threat of termination. If the arrears are not paid within that deadline, it can terminate a residential lease with at least **30 days’ notice to the end of a month**.  
4. **If a landlord’s termination arrives, obtain advice immediately.** Under **Art. 273 OR**, a challenge normally must reach the tenancy conciliation authority within **30 days of receipt**. A valid termination for rent arrears cannot be extended on hardship grounds under **Art. 272a OR**, even though you have a young child.

**Bottom line:** he cannot simply walk away from the joint lease, but you should not leave the rent partly unpaid while arguing about his share. Secure payment and clarify his notice first; then negotiate a sole-tenancy agreement and resolve rent contributions and child maintenance separately.
